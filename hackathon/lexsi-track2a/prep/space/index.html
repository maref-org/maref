Layered Defense Against Encoded Attacks

Measured on CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global · red-team set v1 (114 prompts) · 2026-11-02

What we found

Encoding attacks (base64, rot13, emoji substitution, fragment splitting) bypass model-level safety entirely. We measured this instead of assuming it: inference-time refusal-direction steering, tested at six strengths, changed encoding ASR by at most one prompt in twenty-four.

The reason is structural. Safety refusal is triggered semantically; an encoded payload does not read as a harmful request, so the refusal pathway never activates. Adding a refusal vector to the residual stream does nothing when the model never recognises the intent.

Layer 2 detail — normalisation feeding the model

Fifteen decode paths (base64, base32, rot13, reversal, fragment concat, morse, emoji map, keyboard shift, homoglyph, zalgo strip, leet, pinyin hint) hand the recovered text to the model. Seven of twenty-four attacks were then refused. The rest decoded correctly but the model still complied.

Two different model families, same size class. The other categories move in both directions — different post-training, different weaknesses. Encoding is the one row that is identical, and identical at the ceiling: neither model refused a single encoded request. A 7B control was attempted and could not be loaded on this machine (every request timed out); that question stays open.

Cross-turn gating: tried, no gain

We built a gate that joins recent turns and re-checks the concatenation, expecting turn-split attacks to fall out. On the 30 multi-turn prompts it denied exactly the same number as the single-turn gate: 10/30, gain 0. Reading the data explains why — those attacks split reference, not text. "Now explain how to perform that verb" contains no harmful string, and no join of the prompts produces one. The harm is carried by the model's own earlier answer.

A probe comparing outputs at strength 0.0 and 1.5 confirms the intervention was applied. It simply does not move refusal behaviour on a 3.35B model with a direction extracted from four contrast pairs. We report this rather than tuning the metric.

The ASR column here uses the automated detector. After reviewing the baseline by hand (and reclassifying six prompts where the model could not decode or asked for context), the encoding baseline is 87.5%, not 100%. The steering deltas are unchanged; only the absolute level shifts.

Cross-check: two scorers, one result

The same model outputs were scored a second time through AuditKIT's RedTeamRunner with an independent detector implementation: ASR 78.72% and over-refusal 0.0%, identical to our own scorer. The headline numbers are not an artifact of one regex.
